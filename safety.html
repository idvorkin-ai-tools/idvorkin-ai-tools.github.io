Safety and alignment, mapped

Eight field maps in this org are about making AI systems behave: what the field is, how a model is given values, whose instructions it should follow, what it may do with what it knows, and how to test it. They were built separately and now link into each other. This page says what question each answers, suggests an order, and shows how they connect. Back to all maps.

How they connect

A reading order

  1. Get the lay of the land: AI Safety, then Technical AI Safety for the course-length version.
  2. How a model gets its values: Constitutional AI, the 2022 paper and Claude's 2026 constitution.
  3. Whose instructions count: Instruction hierarchy, which bridges back to the constitution.
  4. What an agent may do with information: Agentic privacy, then CIMemories for the memory case.
  5. How to test all of it: HarmBench and the red-teaming ramp-up.

The maps

An engineer's orientation to alignment, interpretability, evaluations and governance: the subfields, who works on what, and an entry point into each.

Technical AI Safety, Mapped

BlueDot Impact's course in six units and twenty-seven chapters, with every reading linked and every exercise written out: RLHF, scalable oversight, interpretability, evaluation.

Constitutional AI, Mapped

How do you train a model from a written set of values?

Anthropic's 2022 paper (self-critique and RL from AI feedback) section by section, then Claude's 2026 constitution chapter by chapter: ranked core values, principals, honesty, harm, hard constraints, corrigibility.

Claude's Constitution, a Ladder

What does Claude's 2026 constitution actually ask, and why?

Six levels from least to most understanding, each part noting what changed since 2023 and why, with six explorables for the priority order, who can change what, honesty, harm, corrigibility and the change map.

Instruction Hierarchy, Mapped

When instructions conflict, whose wins?

The papers that rank system, user and tool text (Wallace et al. 2024, segment embeddings, reasoning-based training, many-tier hierarchies), plus three bridges: to contextual integrity, to the constitution's principals, and to memory.

Agentic Privacy, Mapped

What should an agent be allowed to do with what it can reach?

The CAPS workshop report: contextual integrity applied to AI agents, why least privilege breaks, the contextual policy engine, and all 51 open problems.

CIMemories, Mapped

Does an assistant with memory tell the right things to the right people?

A benchmark of remembered facts and recipients: every model tested leaks, leaks accumulate with use, and privacy prompts turn everything down at once.

HarmBench, Mapped

How do you measure whether attacks get through?

The standard automated red-teaming benchmark by harm category, behavior type and attack method, with what the attack-success number hides.

Red Teaming Models — A Ramp-Up Map

Threat models, attack families, evaluations and how the field is organized, for security professionals moving into adversarial AI testing.
